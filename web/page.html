      Reading the numbers

      Each target is scored in all three modes you can put in front of the sensor. Full spectrum is the camera with no UV/IR cut at all; visible is a UV/IR-cut filter, the colour-correct baseline; duo-band passes only Hα and O III (a 7 nm narrowband on a mono camera, where shooting one line at a time costs nothing). SkyQual is the signal-to-noise each mode achieves, where 1.00 is a visible frame under this site’s moonless sky — so the tick on every bar is the same reference. Sub is the exposure at which sky shot noise swamps read noise, Robin Glover’s criterion at a 5% stack-noise penalty.

      Full spectrum is a wash, not a shortcut

      Dropping the UV/IR cut opens ~1.6× more sky but only ~1.3× more target: the near-IR is where the OH airglow bands live. The +2% SNR does not pay for the colour cast and the bloated stars, which is why the standard advice holds.

      On emission targets the filter beats the drive

      A duo-band passes the nebula’s line flux while cutting continuum sky ~20×. That margin is the same at Bortle 8 as at Bortle 3 — owning the filter can be worth more than the drive to a dark site.

      An unmodified camera is the real bottleneck

      A stock DSLR’s built-in IR cut passes only about 20% of Hα, so a duo-band bolted to one is mostly an O III filter. Pick the stock DSLR above and watch every emission nebula’s numbers collapse.
